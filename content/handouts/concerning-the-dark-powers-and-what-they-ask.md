# Concerning the Dark Powers, and What They Ask

<div class="official-record">
Nine propositions, with no answers appended.<br>
Written by a priest who would not put his name to it.
</div>

<div class="seal">
WALPOLE & SON · VALLAKI<br>
One volume, bound. Paid in full.<br>
Entered the ninth of Sintyavr, in the year 723.<br>
To be carried out to the manor at Barovia.
</div>

## Preface

I was asked by my abbot to write an instruction for ordinary people, so that they would know the thing when it came to them. I have failed at the instruction and written this instead: nine things I believe I can defend, and nothing after them.

I have not signed it. My name would be the first thing given away, and I have read enough now to be careful about what I give.

<div class="book-spread">

<div class="book-page">

### I

The people of this valley call them the Mists, and blame them for the frost, the wolves, a bad birth and a lost shoe alike.

A people who blame everything on one thing have stopped looking at anything.

### II

They are not native to this valley. Every account I have gathered agrees on that, and on nothing else whatever.

### III

**They do not take.** This is the proposition I would stake my order on. In every account I have been able to read — and I have read badly translated ones twice, to be sure of it — what was given was *offered first*.

### IV

They do not haggle. The price is named, or it is not named, and either way it is exact. I find the unnamed price the more honest of the two, and I cannot explain why.

</div>

<div class="book-page">

### V

<div class="whisper">
Never, in any account, does the price fall on the one who asked.
</div>

*Beside this proposition the margin is scored through three times, and a second hand has written:*

<div class="corruption">
Then let it be me. Let it be me. I will write it down and I will say it aloud: let it be me.
</div>

### VI

They do not lie. My accounts are unanimous, and this is the worst sentence in my book. The gift is precisely the thing that was asked for — no less than it, and never anything other than it.

A liar can be caught. There is no catching an honest one.

### VII

I have three accounts of men who refused. All three lived out their lives.

All three were asked again.

</div>

</div>

<div class="book-spread">

<div class="book-page">

### VIII

They are patient on a scale that ought to comfort us. A thing that can wait four hundred years for an answer is in no hurry to ruin you this winter.

It does not comfort me. Patience is not mercy. Patience is only mercy's shape, worn by something that has all the time there is.

### IX

I have prayed over this book for a year and a day. The Morninglord has not answered me on it.

I do not take silence for absence. I take it for an answer I am not yet fit to be given.

</div>

<div class="book-page">

### What I Could Not Write

My abbot wanted instruction. Here is the nearest I have.

If ever a thing speaks to you — out of an object, out of a dream, out of a door that should not be there — and offers you something shaped exactly to the size of what you want, do not begin by asking what it costs. You will be told the truth and it will not help you.

**Ask who pays.**

Ask it out loud. Ask it standing in front of someone who loves you, in the light, where they can hear the question and watch you ask it.

If you find that you cannot do that, then you already have your answer, and you had it before you opened this book.

</div>
